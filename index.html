01 · Motivation

Why Football
& Economy?

Turkish football history features iconic peaks — Galatasaray's 2000 UEFA Cup victory and UEFA Super Cup, Beşiktaş becoming the first and only Turkish club to top their Champions League group unbeaten (2009–10), and the national team's historic 3rd-place finish at the 2002 World Cup — Turkey's best ever international result. These moments coincided with periods of relative economic stability in Turkey. Was this a coincidence?

This project investigates whether macroeconomic conditions — exchange rates, GDP growth, and inflation — meaningfully correlate with Turkish football clubs' UEFA performance and the national team's FIFA ranking. To enable cross-country comparison and increase statistical power for machine learning, England's Premier League Big 6 and the England national team were added as a structural counterpoint.

H₁ — Alternative Hypothesis

Stronger economic conditions are positively associated with better international football performance (ρ > 0, one-tailed, α = 0.05).

H₀ — Null Hypothesis

There is no statistically significant linear correlation between economic indicators and football success (ρ = 0).

02 · Data

Two Countries,
128 Data Points

Each country contributes 64 half-year periods (H1: January–June, H2: July–December), resulting in a combined dataset of 128 observations used for machine learning. A composite Economic Health Index was constructed by min-max normalising and averaging three indicators: exchange rate (inverted for Turkey, direct for England), GDP growth, and inflation (inverted).

03 · Methodology

Four-Phase
Analysis

01

EDA & Visualisation

Time-series plots of exchange rates, per-club UEFA coefficients, national team FIFA index, and the composite economic health index for both countries.

02

Hypothesis Testing

Pearson r (one-tailed, α=0.05) with 95% Fisher CI, Spearman ρ as non-parametric backup, and normality checks via D'Agostino K² test.

03

OLS Regression

Ordinary Least Squares regressing total club UEFA points on economic index, GDP growth, and CPI inflation to quantify explanatory power (R²).

04

Machine Learning

K-Means clustering (k=3, elbow method), PCA visualisation, and three supervised classifiers with 5-fold stratified cross-validation.

TR vs ENG Classification

Random Forest perfectly distinguishes Turkey from England using only economic and football features. This confirms the two economies are structurally distinct — a strong pound and stable inflation produce very different footballing outcomes than a volatile lira and high inflation.

K-Means clustering (k=3, elbow method): three natural eras identified — 1990s hyper-inflation era (low UEFA points), 2000s stability era (peak Turkish football), and 2020s high-currency era (mixed results). PCA explained 57% of variance in 2 components.

06 · Conclusions

What Did
We Find?

🇹🇷

Turkey — Club Level: H₁ Supported

A statistically significant positive correlation (r = +0.225, p = 0.037) between economic health and club UEFA performance was found. A stronger Turkish economy enables clubs to pay for better foreign players and reduce debt, translating into European success.

📊

OLS Explains 61%

Economic variables (exchange rate, GDP growth, inflation) explain 60.9% of the variance in Turkish club UEFA points — a strong result for macroeconomic predictors in sport. The F-test is highly significant (F = 31.13, p < 0.001).

🏴󠁧󠁢󠁥󠁮󠁧󠁿

England — Decoupled from Economy

England's Big 6 clubs showed a negative (non-significant) correlation. Premier League revenue from global broadcast rights and foreign ownership (e.g. Abu Dhabi's Man City purchase in 2008) decouples club success from domestic economic conditions.

🤖

ML: 128 Rows = 90.7% Accuracy

Combining Turkey and England to 128 rows — as suggested by the course instructor — raised classification accuracy from ~69% to 90.7%. The two countries are structurally so different that the model predicts country of origin with 100% accuracy.

🏆

Golden Era Explained

Galatasaray's 2000 UEFA Cup, Beşiktaş's unbeaten UCL group top finish (2009-10), and Turkey's 2002 World Cup 3rd place all align with periods of relative economic stability in Turkey (lower inflation, moderate exchange rates). The data supports this narrative quantitatively.

⚠️

Inflation — Unexpected Direction

High inflation showed a large negative correlation with club success (r = −0.590) — the strongest single finding. This makes economic sense: hyperinflation (1990s Turkey) destroys clubs' purchasing power for foreign players.

07 · Limitations & Future Work

What Could
Be Better?

  • N = 64 per country is below the ideal for machine learning. The combined N = 128 partially addresses this, as flagged by the course instructor (Neşe Hoca).
  • GDP and CPI are annual figures assigned to both half-years of the same year, reducing temporal granularity.
  • Per-club half-year UEFA points are estimates reconstructed from archival data; exact values should be verified against primary UEFA sources.
  • Confounders not captured: manager changes, transfer policy, club ownership changes, and individual player quality.
  • Future work could incorporate Transfermarkt squad value data, adding a direct monetary measure of club investment alongside economic indicators.
  • Extending to other leagues (Germany, Spain, France) would allow a true multi-country panel analysis with much larger N.
